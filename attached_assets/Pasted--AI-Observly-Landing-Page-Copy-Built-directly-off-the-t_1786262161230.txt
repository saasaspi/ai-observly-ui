**Closing line:** Total bill is one number. MRR is another. AI Observly is the bridge between them.

**Section header:** From "here's the invoice" to "here's the margin"

Body copy:
AI Observly sits between your LLM provider and your product, tagging every request with a customer_id, a feature, and a plan. Instead of one flat number at the end of the month, you get a live P&L for your AI spend — who costs you the most, what it costs to run each feature, and whether each pricing tier is actually profitable.

## 4. Core Features

**Section header:** Built for the questions your invoice can't answer

**Card 1 — Per-Customer Cost Attribution**
Every API call gets mapped to the customer_id that triggered it. Instantly see your top spenders, find the small slice of users eating most of your bill, and catch a margin-negative account before it costs you a full quarter.

**Card 2 — Per-Feature Margins & ROI**
See exactly what each AI feature costs to run against what it earns you. Spot the feature that's a genuine cash cow, and the one that's technically "used" but quietly losing money on every invocation — so you know what to double down on and what to re-scope or retire.

**Card 3 — Plan & Pricing Profitability**
Break down AI cost by pricing tier. See which plans generate enough revenue to cover the AI cost they create, and which ones are being subsidized by your other customers — so your next pricing change is based on actual unit economics, not a guess.

**Card 4 — Trial & Free-Tier Cost Tracking**
Isolate how much of your total AI spend is going to trial and free-tier users before they ever convert. Set usage guardrails with real numbers instead of finding out after the invoice lands.

## 5. How It Works

**Section header:** Three steps from raw invoice to real margin

**Step 1 — Connect**
Point AI Observly at your OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini usage. Attach a customer_id, feature tag, and plan to your existing calls — no rebuild required.

**Step 2 — Attribute**
Every request is automatically mapped to the customer, feature, and plan that generated it. No spreadsheets, no manual tagging after the fact.

**Step 3 — Decide**
Your dashboard surfaces cost, margin, and ROI at the customer, feature, and plan level — so pricing, roadmap, and "who do we talk to about their usage" decisions are based on data, not a hunch.

## 7. FAQ

**"Isn't this the same as the usage dashboard my provider already gives me?"**
No. Your provider dashboard shows you total tokens and total spend. It has no idea which customer, feature, or plan generated that spend — that mapping has to happen on your side, which is exactly what AI Observly does automatically.

**"Do I need to rebuild anything to set this up?"**
No. If you can pass a customer_id (or similar identifier) alongside your existing API calls, you can get customer- and feature-level breakdowns without a rebuild.

**"What LLM providers do you support?"**
OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini today, with more being added.

**"Can this actually help with pricing, or just reporting?"**
Both. Once you can see which plans and which customers are margin-negative, you have the numbers to reprice a tier, add a usage cap, or have a direct conversation with a specific account — instead of raising prices across the board and hoping it fixes itself.

# Suggested edits to the existing site (beyond new copy)

1. **Kill all latency/uptime/API-error language.** If any current section mentions monitoring for latency, errors, or reliability, cut it — it dilutes the "margin visibility" positioning and doesn't match what your ICP is actually asking for on Reddit.
2. **Replace whatever the current hero says with the cost/margin framing above.** The Reddit threads show people arriving with a very specific mental model ("total bill vs. per-customer black box") — the hero should mirror that language back to them in the first five seconds.
3. **Lead with the pain, not the product.** Put the "Sound familiar?" section directly under the hero, before any feature list. People self-select in once they see their exact problem described.
4. **Make the 80/20 framing a visual, not just a line of copy.** A simple bar chart mockup ("Top 10% of customers = 60% of AI spend") on the hero or problem section would do more than any headline — it's the exact "aha" moment the first Redditor described.
5. **Add a pricing-decision angle somewhere visible**, not buried in features — several of the Reddit questions are really pricing questions ("which plans cover their AI cost"), so a specific "Pricing & Margin" mention in the nav or hero subhead will resonate.
6. **Consider a short "built after reading these questions" note or FAQ entry** that's honest about the origin — B2B SaaS founders trust "we built this because we had the same blind spot" more than generic feature marketing.
7. Keep your existing "Start monitoring now" CTA — it already fits this positioning well, no change needed there.
